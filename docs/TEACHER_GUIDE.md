# Teacher guide · Expanding Universe

## Purpose and use

This simulator provides an individual investigation when a class has too few students or too few physical supplies. It preserves prediction, repeated measurement, recording, calculation, graph interpretation, and evidence-based explanation.

It is an independent implementation of a common balloon expansion model. The protocol below is the protocol implemented here; compare it with your licensed Hallock worksheet before assigning exact question numbers. This site does not distribute or claim to reproduce the commercial worksheet, video, or answer key.

Use an optional student code rather than a full name. Student responses stay in the browser unless the user downloads and shares them. Save a work file before clearing data or changing devices.

## Procedure and supports

| Step | Student task | Adult support |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 · Predict | Add A–J to a 10 cm balloon. Predict how distances will change and explain why. | Read one direction at a time. Accept any reasoned prediction. |
| 2 · First measurements | Inflate to 20 cm. Select B–J, lay the surface tape from A, round, and record nine distances. | Model how to read a tape and round using the separate example. Keep the student's actual entries. |
| 3 · Second measurements | Inflate to 30 cm and repeat the same nine pairs. | Point out that the marks stay at the same locations on the growing surface. |
| 4 · Calculate | Subtract first from second; divide by 8 model years; multiply that rate by 24 and 32. | Use the built-in calculator, written formulas, and a short break between rows. Preserve all nine comparisons. |
| 5 · Explain | Compare two galaxies and connect the pattern to expansion, then identify a limitation. | Use sentence frames, device dictation, or scribe the student's own oral explanation. |

There is no timer and no automatic submission. Use short sessions and return to saved work. All directions are visual; sound is not required. Controls work with a keyboard. The chart's exact numbers are available in the table. Teacher response checks are still necessary: the app checks numeric entries but does not grade written scientific reasoning.

The 10 cm balloon is a setup stage, not a third data-collection stage. The graph plots **distance at the 20 cm stage** on x and **model rate** on y. The final two columns are **additional motion over a duration at a constant rate**, not total distance from A and not observed future astronomical data.

Use up to three decimal places for rates so values such as 0.375 are retained.

## Suggested explanations

1. **Observation:** The galaxies became farther apart.
2. **Claim:** Galaxies that started farther from A generally had larger model recession rates.
3. **Evidence:** B began 3 cm from A and had a rate of 0.25 cm/model year. J began 22 cm from A and had a rate of 1.25 cm/model year.
4. **Reasoning:** The balloon's surface expanded. Fixed locations on the surface became more separated. The marks did not need to crawl across the surface.
5. **Connection:** Growing surface distances model increasing distances in an expanding universe. The distance–rate trend resembles the large-scale pattern inferred from galaxy distances and redshifts. The model itself does not observe or measure redshift.
6. **Limitation:** Accept one accurate limitation, such as a two-dimensional surface modeling three-dimensional space; arbitrary centimeter and year scales; no spectrum or cosmic microwave background evidence; or an idealized uniform expansion that does not model local gravity.

Equivalent student wording is acceptable. A correct prediction is not required; an explanation revised using evidence is useful scientific work. A filled response field alone does not establish understanding.

Helpful prompts: “Which value is bigger?” → “Show me two rows.” → “What stretched between the markers?” Avoid giving the entire conclusion before the student examines the data.

## How the model works

Galaxies occupy fixed unit vectors on a sphere. A pair's shortest surface distance is:

**distance = (diameter ÷ 2) × arccos(clamped dot product of the two unit vectors)**

This is a surface arc, not a straight chord through the balloon. The curved tape is rendered on that same arc. An unrolled tape and numeric reading support measurement.

The camera turns toward the selected pair so its tape is visible; this camera motion does not change galaxy positions or distances. Enlarging the diameter from 20 to 30 cm multiplies every exact pairwise surface distance by 1.5. Recorded whole centimeters can deviate from that ratio after rounding. Marker size stays readable and does not represent galaxy growth.

The balloon's surface has no preferred center point. A is a chosen reference galaxy. The balloon's three-dimensional center, inside, outside, and inflation source are not part of the analogy. Do not describe the Big Bang as galaxies exploding out from A into a pre-existing empty room. The model does not imply that the universe has the balloon's global shape or an edge. Bound objects such as galaxies do not expand with this classroom model.

The assumed 8-year interval is a calculation scale, not elapsed student work time, the universe's age, or a measured cosmic expansion rate. Constant-rate projections are an exercise; they do not capture the actual expansion history.

## Standards and laboratory use

**Partial support for NYSSLS / NGSS HS-ESS1-2.** Students develop and use a model, analyze mathematical data, identify a scale-related pattern, and construct an explanation from evidence. These are relevant practices and concepts; this activity alone does not assess the entire performance expectation.

HS-ESS1-2 calls for an explanation of the Big Bang using astronomical evidence. A full sequence must also address light spectra and redshift, the motion of distant galaxies, cosmic microwave background radiation, and the composition of the early universe. Add those sources of evidence and a separate synthesis assessment. This lab's explanatory paragraph introduces the redshift connection without pretending the balloon generates spectra.

New York guidance allows combinations of hands-on and simulated laboratory experiences with satisfactory reports for the applicable laboratory requirement. Acceptance of a particular experience, satisfactory completion, accommodations, and minutes must be determined by the teacher and school using current NYSED guidance. Required state science investigations must still be completed according to their own instructions. This app does not certify credit and is not an approved replacement for them.

The report includes prediction, method, all measurements and calculations, graph, explanations, and space for teacher review and actual accepted minutes. The 8 model years must never be counted as instructional time.
