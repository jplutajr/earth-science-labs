Help me round

Look at the first digit after the decimal point. If it is 5 or more, round up. If it is less than 5, round down.

Example: 6.4 cm → 6 cm. 6.5 cm → 7 cm.

We round the model's distance, so small differences can become ties in the table.

Change ÷ Time = Rate

In this activity: Change ÷ 8 years = Rate

The distance can be different for each galaxy. The time is always 8 model years.

Example: Galaxy B

5 − 3 = 2 cm
2 ÷ 8 = 0.25 cm/year

Keep up to 3 decimal places. Predictions use the same rate. They show extra distance, not total distance.

Connect to real evidence

Light from most distant galaxies is shifted toward longer, redder wavelengths. Together with distance measurements, this supports a pattern: more distant galaxies generally recede faster. Our balloon models that pattern; it does not measure light.

Words and ideas to help

Expand: grow so distances increase.
Rate: change in distance for each unit of time.
Evidence: data that support an explanation.
Model: a useful representation with limits.

The universe has three dimensions of space. This lab uses a two-dimensional surface. A is a chosen reference point; it is not the center of the universe. The balloon's inside and outside are not parts of the analogy.

The fixed-size markers help track positions. Real galaxies do not inflate like the balloon. The model does not show cosmic microwave background radiation, early hydrogen and helium, or gravitationally bound systems.
